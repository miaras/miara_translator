# Shiroko: first-customer campaign

Draft v1 · 2026-11-01

## Decision

Test English-speaking readers of Korean books as the first audience. Position Shiroko around following a book with sentence-by-sentence English support. This is a hypothesis based on product capabilities, not validated customer demand.

Assumptions: founder-operated, limited marketing budget, no verified acquisition or retention baseline. No campaigns have been launched and no product changes are included in this brief.

## Product context

Shiroko is a subscription translation workspace with 58 target languages, aligned editable sentences, PDF/Word import, saved translations, existing-draft improvement, and a paid book-photo transcription workspace. Current monthly plans are $9, $19, and $99. English–Korean human review is a separate service.

The initial job to test: “Help me work through a Korean book while keeping the original visible.” Secondary audiences to investigate later: tutors preparing bilingual reading materials and translators revising long drafts. These audiences have different buying reasons and should have separate campaigns.

Potential friction: switching between a book, camera translation, dictionaries, and text editors. This is an interview hypothesis; no customer quotes or measured time savings are available.

Alternatives to investigate in interviews: camera translation, general chat assistants, document translators, bilingual reading tools, and waiting for a published translation. The proposed advantage is the combined workflow of page import, editable transcription, aligned translation, and revision. No exclusive-feature or quality-superiority claim has been established.

Brand direction: clear, calm, concrete. Existing copy includes “Read in context.” Proposed copy should name the material and show the output. Avoid unsupported accuracy claims, invented testimonials, and language-learning outcome promises.

Proof available: working product features and recorded demonstrations. Proof missing: customer outcomes, comparative quality tests, conversion rates, retention, willingness to pay, and customer acquisition costs. No customer-language research has been supplied.

Likely objections to test: “Why pay when I already use a free translator?”, “Can I trust this sentence?”, and “How much of my book will my allowance cover?” Answer with a real workflow demonstration, inspectable source/translation pairs, and an explicit usage estimate. Do not promise full-book coverage based on text-only page estimates: photo transcription also consumes allowance.

Switching hypothesis: readers want easier comparison but already know their existing tools and may hesitate to upload material or subscribe. Explain what is sent for processing and make a sample available before asking them to switch.

## Campaign copy

Headline: **Read Korean books with English beside you.**

Subhead: **Turn photos of your book into editable text and an English translation. Follow the original sentence by sentence, and refine passages as you go.**

Current-product CTA: **See how it works** → existing demonstration.

Purchase CTA: **Start with Personal — $9/month.** Explain allowance and photo-transcription usage beside the purchase decision.

Proposed experiment, requiring implementation: a small capped photo trial. Only advertise “Try your first page” after this is supported. The current photo workspace requires a paid subscription.

## Four experiments, in priority order

### 1. Guided first reading sessions

Why: tests whether the workflow solves a recurring problem before increasing traffic.

Founder tasks: recruit 15 people who already own a Korean book; observe a short first session; follow up after seven days. Ask what they used before, where they got stuck, what they trusted, and whether they voluntarily brought a second passage.

Draft invitation: “I’m building Shiroko for reading Korean books with English beside the original. If you already have a book you’re working through, I’d love to watch you try a short passage and hear what feels useful or frustrating. Would you be interested in a 15-minute session?”

Resources: approximately 6–8 founder hours plus explicitly budgeted product access. Recruitment messages are drafts, not sent.

Learning target: five of 15 voluntarily use another passage within seven days, and three pay after understanding the price. These are internal decision rules, not market benchmarks. If readers need repeated prompting, investigate usefulness before recruiting more.

### 2. Demonstration clips

Why: the physical-book-to-aligned-text transformation is visible and specific.

Create three clips from one real session, using owned or public-domain material. Test these hooks separately:

- “Have a Korean book you can’t quite read yet?”
- “Keep the Korean sentence beside the English.”
- “Here’s how I work through a difficult Korean paragraph.”

20-second script: show book and hook (0–3s); import a photo and show transcription (3–8s); show aligned English and Korean (8–14s); inspect and edit one sentence (14–18s); finish with “See Shiroko in action” and the URL (18–20s). Label any sped-up processing.

Publish six posts over two weeks on one account where the founder can participate consistently. Track each hook with a distinct campaign link. Compare qualified visits and completed first translations; views are supporting evidence.

Resources: approximately 4–6 hours using the existing recordings and product. Do not assume a paid ad budget.

### 3. Tutor and creator partnerships

Why: tutors can show the tool inside a reading task their audience already understands.

Identify ten tutors or creators who regularly demonstrate Korean reading. Personalize each invitation around an actual lesson. Offer a walkthrough and access with a clear usage cap; let them evaluate it before discussing a paid placement.

Draft: “Your lesson on [specific passage/topic] caught my attention. I’m building a tool that puts Korean book text and editable English translations side by side. Would you like to try it with a short passage from one of your lessons? I’d value your honest feedback on whether it helps your students follow the original.”

Resources: approximately 3–4 hours plus capped access. Initial learning target: two evaluations and one independently chosen demonstration. Count activated readers and subsequent paid customers by partner.

Existing referral links reward new-account signups. They are attribution aids, not proof of purchase or retention, and are not an affiliate commission system.

### 4. One useful search tutorial

Why: tests whether people looking for this workflow find the product useful. Search volume is unverified; do not extrapolate traffic.

Publish “How to read a Korean book with a side-by-side English translation.” Include page preparation, transcription correction, sentence comparison, a difficult passage, and actual usage costs. Reuse clips and screenshots from experiment 2.

Resources: approximately 4 hours. Track search impressions, relevant visits, sample engagement, and first translations over 6–8 weeks. Treat this as a slower experiment; no early traffic is inconclusive.

## Activation and measurement

Primary outcome: weekly readers who translate a passage and return on another day to work on another passage. Instrumentation is proposed, not verified as implemented.

Track acquisition source, signup, transcription completion/failure, translation completion/failure, a meaningful follow-up action such as editing or exporting, repeat passage on a later day, paid conversion, and renewal. Keep document contents out of analytics.

Define seven-day return as a second passage on a later day within seven days of first translation. Only include users whose full seven-day window has ended in the return-rate denominator. Report sample sizes and pending users. Deduplicate purchases by verified order ID.

Interview readers who finish a first passage but do not return. Distinguish a completed one-time task from an unusable product; the former may favor prepaid usage over a recurring reading proposition.

Before paid acquisition, establish contribution margin after processing, payment fees, refunds, and support. Choose a maximum acquisition cost from conservative contribution over an explicit payback period. Subscription price alone is not an acquisition budget.

## First month

Week 1: recruit first five readers, observe sessions, record baseline friction, prepare campaign copy and usage explanation.

Week 2: finish 15 sessions, publish first three clips, contact first five tutor prospects.

Week 3: publish remaining clips, follow up on mature reader cohorts, contact remaining tutors, draft tutorial.

Week 4: review repeat use, payments, objections, and source quality. Continue the strongest audience/message pair. If interest is high but first translations fail, fix activation. If first translations succeed but repeat use is weak, reassess the recurring job and packaging.
